    Databases: SQL

    The project involved building an SQL database for a barber shop. Its main purpose is to manage the services offered by the barbers and to store information about those services, the certificates and courses the barbers have completed, the client base and their visits, as well as the products sold in the shop and the orders placed.

    Database functionality:

  • Listing all visits with barber and client details, sorted by visit date in descending order.
  • Listing all orders with product names and prices, sorted by order date.
  • Counting the number of transactions per client, sorted by client ID, first name and surname.
  • Finding the highest and lowest transaction amount.
  • Listing the names of clients who bought the product called shaving cream.
  • Listing products priced above the average price of all products.
  • Listing clients who visited the same barber more than once.

    Methods and techniques of business decision making

    The project consisted of two parts: building an econometric model and an optimisation model.

    Part 1: Econometric model

    The first part examined how the number of people employed in a given sector affects the average monthly salary. The analysis used statistical data covering 2010 to 2022, collected among others from government sources. The econometric model was built in Gretl, which supported the data analysis and regression. The model aimed to identify the relationship between headcount and pay, which can help in understanding the dynamics of the labour market in a sector.

    Part 2: Optimisation model

    The second part focused on an optimisation model for the production of screws and rivets, aimed at maximising company revenue. The model was meant to indicate the optimal production decisions given the available resources and the constraints affecting production efficiency, which made it possible to propose changes that increase profit through process optimisation.

    IT system implementation concept

    The project involved carrying out a pre-implementation analysis of an information system, in my case an ERP and CRM system, for a company producing and selling clothing online.

    Goals of the analysis

    The main goal was to assess the feasibility of implementing a system that would automate the company's key processes, such as production, inventory and customer service. The analysis focused on the potential benefits, risks and costs of implementing integrated ERP and CRM systems.

    The analysis covered a detailed study of the following processes:

  • Production management: from production planning through to quality control and deliveries.
  • Inventory management: optimising stock levels and automating orders.
  • Customer service: improving communication with the customer, order management and after-sales support.

    • Conclusions

    The analysis established that the company had not used any ERP or CRM system so far, which meant that most processes were handled manually, without automated support. Introducing an integrated ERP and CRM system would significantly improve operational efficiency and customer service.
